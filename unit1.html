💡 文化洞见 Key Insight
"How are you?" is NOT a real question in America — it's just a greeting ritual.
The expected answer is NOT a detailed life update.
Say: "Good, thanks!" and move on.
If you give a long honest answer, people will be confused and uncomfortable.
"How are you?" 在美国不是真正的问题，只是打招呼的仪式。正确回答不是描述你的真实状态，而是说 "Good, thanks!" 然后继续。如果你给出长篇大论的真实回答，对方会困惑尴尬。

💡 文化洞见 Key Insight
Americans introduce themselves with FIRST NAME only.
"I'm Chen Wei" sounds formal. "I'm Wei" sounds natural.

💡 文化洞见 Key Insight
Saying "I don't understand" and going silent is the WORST option.
It makes you seem disengaged. Native speakers appreciate when you ask for clarification — it shows you're trying.
"Sorry, I didn't quite catch that" sounds more natural than "I don't understand."
Most people are HAPPY to repeat or explain. Just ask.
听不懂就沉默是最糟糕的选择。这让你显得不在意。母语者很欣赏你请求澄清——这说明你在努力。"Sorry, I didn't quite catch that" 比 "I don't understand" 更地道。大多数人很乐意重复或解释，只要你开口问。

💡 文化洞见 Key Insight
Americans often "tip" (小费) 18-20% at restaurants — it's not optional, it's expected.
At coffee shops, there's usually a tip screen — 15-20% is standard.
Also: "Can I get..." is more common than "I would like..." or "I want..." when ordering.
And "To go" = takeaway. "For here" = dine in.
美国餐厅通常需要给小费（tip）18-20%——这不是可选的，是期望的。咖啡馆通常有小费屏幕，15-20%是标准。另外，点餐时 "Can I get..." 比 "I would like..." 或 "I want..." 更常用。"To go" = 带走，"For here" = 堂食。

💡 文化洞见 Key Insight
American prices on tags DON'T include tax. The final price at checkout will be higher.
Sales tax varies by state — roughly 6-10% added at checkout.
Also: stores rarely haggle (讨价还价).
"Is there any discount?" works, but "Can you lower the price?" sounds out of place in most American stores.
Outlets and flea markets are the exception.
美国标签上的价格不含税。收银台的最终价格会更高。销售税因州而异，大约6-10%在结账时加上。另外，商店很少讨价还价。"有折扣吗？" 可以问，但在大多数美国商店说"能便宜点吗？"会显得格格不入。折扣店和跳蚤市场例外。

💡 文化洞见 Key Insight
In the US, you usually need an APPOINTMENT to see a doctor (not walk-in).
For urgent non-emergency situations: "Urgent Care" clinics are walk-in and cheaper than ER.
For true emergencies: go to the ER (Emergency Room).
Always bring your insurance card. If you don't have insurance, ask for "self-pay" rates.

💡 文化洞见 Key Insight
5 unwritten American social rules you must know:
1. Personal space: don't stand too close when talking.
2. "How are you?" = hello. Don't actually answer it.
3. Tipping: 18-20% at restaurants. Not optional.
4. First names always — even with bosses. "Mr. Smith" sounds very formal.
5. Vague invitations ("let's hang out!") are usually just small talk, not real plans.
Bonus: Smile and make eye contact — it's friendly, not aggressive.
