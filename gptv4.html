📐 Grammar Lessons & Exercises
I am... · Describing Yourself
Use "I am" to say who you are or how you feel.
I am happy. 🔊
I am a student. 🔊
I am seven years old. 🔊
I am from China. 🔊
💡 Contraction: I am → I'm (I'm happy.)
Practice Exercises
___ am a student.
I ___ happy today.
I am ___ years old. (write your age)
I'm ___ from a big city.
I can... · Talking About Ability
Use "I can" to say what you are able to do.
I can run fast. 🔊
I can read books. 🔊
I can swim. 🔊
I cannot fly. (= I can't fly.) 🔊
💡 "Can" does NOT change. Never say "I cans" ✗
Practice Exercises
I ___ jump high.
She ___ sing well.
I can't ___ (swim / swims).
___ you speak English?
This is... / That is... · Pointing to Things
"This" = near you. "That" = far from you.
This is my book. 🔊
That is a big tree. 🔊
This is not my bag. 🔊
What is this? — It is a cat. 🔊
💡 "This" and "that" are always singular. For plural: These / Those.
Practice Exercises
___ is my pencil. (near me)
___ is a bird in the sky. (far)
___ is an apple or an orange?
___ are my shoes. (near, more than one)
A / An / The · Articles
"A" before consonant sounds. "An" before vowel sounds (a, e, i, o, u). "The" for specific things.
I have a cat. 🔊
She eats an apple. 🔊
The cat is orange. 🔊
I go to the park every day. 🔊
💡 Say "an hour" (not "a hour") because H is silent → sounds like a vowel.
Practice Exercises
I see ___ elephant.
___ sun is bright today.
She has ___ dog and ___ cat.
This is ___ orange. ___ orange is sweet.

My Best Friend ★☆☆ (Grade 1 · ~80 words)
💡 建议先读懂英文原文，遇到不懂的句子点每句旁边的【译】按钮。整段翻译可复制文章后粘贴到 translate.google.com

My best friend is Sam.

We go to the same school.

A Rainy Day ★★☆ (Grade 1 · ~90 words)
💡 建议先读懂英文原文，遇到不懂的句子点每句旁边的【译】按钮。整段翻译可复制文章后粘贴到 translate.google.com

I look at the rain.

Hint: rainy days are not bad

📐 Grammar Lessons & Exercises
Past Tense · What Happened?
Add -ed to regular verbs for the past. Some verbs are irregular (they change completely).
I walked to school. (walk → walked) 🔊
She jumped over the puddle. (jump → jumped) 🔊
We went to the zoo. (go → went) 🔊
He ate a sandwich. (eat → ate) 🔊
They saw a rainbow. (see → saw) 🔊
💡 Common irregular past: go→went, eat→ate, see→saw, run→ran, have→had, say→said, come→came, take→took
Practice Exercises
Yesterday, I ___ (walk) to the park.
She ___ (eat) pizza for dinner.
We ___ (go) to the beach last summer.
He ___ (see) a shooting star last night.
Conjunctions · Connecting Ideas
Use conjunctions to connect two ideas: AND (adds), BUT (contrasts), OR (choice), SO (result), BECAUSE (reason).
I like cats and dogs. 🔊
I am tired, but I will finish my homework. 🔊
Do you want juice or water? 🔊
It was raining, so we stayed inside. 🔊
She studies hard because she wants good grades. 🔊
💡 "Because" answers the question WHY. "So" shows the RESULT.
Practice Exercises
I wanted to go outside ___ it was raining.
I was cold ___ I put on a jacket.
She cried ___ she lost her toy.
Would you like tea ___ coffee?
Plural Nouns · More Than One
Most nouns add -S or -ES. But some are irregular!
cat → cats 🔊
box → boxes 🔊
child → children 🔊
man → men 🔊
woman → women 🔊
mouse → mice 🔊
foot → feet 🔊
tooth → teeth 🔊
sheep → sheep (no change!) 🔊
💡 Words ending in -s, -x, -z, -ch, -sh → add -ES (box→boxes, beach→beaches)
Practice Exercises
I have three ___ (tooth).
The ___ (child) are playing in the yard.
Two ___ (man) walked into the shop.
I see five ___ (fox) in the forest.
Contractions · Short Forms
A contraction joins two words with an apostrophe ('). The apostrophe shows where letters are missing.
I am → I'm 🔊
you are → you're 🔊
he is → he's 🔊
she is → she's 🔊
it is → it's 🔊
we are → we're 🔊
they are → they're 🔊
do not → don't 🔊
cannot → can't 🔊
will not → won't 🔊
💡 It's = it is. Its (no apostrophe) = belonging to it. These are different!
Practice Exercises
___ a beautiful day! (It is)
I ___ like broccoli. (do not)
___ going to be late. (They are)
She ___ come to school today. (will not)

The New Kid ★★☆ (Grade 2 · ~150 words)
💡 建议先读懂英文原文，遇到不懂的句子点每句旁边的【译】按钮。整段翻译可复制文章后粘贴到 translate.google.com

On Monday, a new student came to our class.
Her name was Mia. She had short black hair and a red backpack.
She sat down quietly and didn't say a word.

At lunch, I noticed Mia was sitting alone.
She looked down at her food and didn't smile.
I remembered my first day at this school.
I was scared and lonely too.

I picked up my tray and walked over to her.
"Hi, my name is Jake. Can I sit here?"
Mia looked up, surprised.
"Yes," she said quietly. "Please sit down."

We talked about our favorite books and games.
She liked the same book series as me!
By the end of lunch, we were both laughing.

The next day, Mia waved at me and smiled.
Sometimes the best thing you can do is just say hello.

Comprehension Questions
1. How did Mia look when she first arrived?
Hint: sat quietly, didn't say a word
2. Why did Jake understand how Mia felt?
Hint: remembered his first day
3. What did Jake do at lunch?
Hint: walked over and asked to sit with her
4. What did Jake and Mia discover they had in common?
Hint: same favorite book series
5. What is the lesson of this story?
Hint: sometimes just saying hello can help someone
How Caterpillars Become Butterflies (Non-fiction) ★★☆ (Grade 2 Non-fiction · ~130 words)
💡 建议先读懂英文原文，遇到不懂的句子点每句旁边的【译】按钮。整段翻译可复制文章后粘贴到 translate.google.com

A butterfly begins its life as a tiny egg on a leaf.
When the egg hatches, a small caterpillar comes out.
The caterpillar is very hungry.
It eats leaves all day and night.
As it eats, it grows bigger and bigger.

Next, the caterpillar forms a hard shell called a chrysalis.
Inside the chrysalis, something amazing happens.
The caterpillar slowly changes its body.
This process is called metamorphosis.

After about two weeks, a beautiful butterfly breaks out.
Its wings are wet at first.
It waits for the wings to dry.
Then it flies away into the sky.

A butterfly's life has four stages:
egg → caterpillar → chrysalis → butterfly.
This cycle starts all over again when the butterfly lays new eggs.

Comprehension Questions
1. What does the caterpillar do after it hatches?
Hint: eats leaves all day and night
2. What is a chrysalis?
Hint: a hard shell the caterpillar forms
3. What is metamorphosis?
Hint: the process of the caterpillar changing
4. List the four stages of a butterfly's life.
Hint: egg → caterpillar → chrysalis → butterfly

✍️ Writing Practice
✍️ Write a Paragraph
A paragraph has 3 parts: Topic Sentence (main idea) + Detail Sentences (reasons/examples) + Closing Sentence.
Model Example:
Topic: My favorite season

My favorite season is winter. First, I love playing in the snow. My friends and I build snowmen and have snowball fights. Second, I enjoy drinking hot chocolate by the fireplace. Finally, winter has the best holiday — Chinese New Year! That is why winter is my favorite season.
Your Turn — Use these sentence starters:
My favorite season is ___.
First, I love ___.
Second, I enjoy ___.
Also, I like ___.
That is why ___ is my favorite season.
✍️ Opinion Writing
State your opinion, give TWO reasons, and write a conclusion. Start with: "I think..." or "In my opinion..."
Model Example:
Should students have homework?

I think students should not have too much homework. My first reason is that kids need time to play and rest. Playing outside helps our brains relax. My second reason is that too much homework can make students feel stressed and unhappy. Students learn better when they are happy. For these reasons, I believe homework should be short and fun.
Your Turn — Use these sentence starters:
I think ___.
My first reason is ___.
My second reason is ___.
For these reasons, I believe ___.

Complex Sentences · Because, Although, When, If
A complex sentence has a main clause + a subordinate clause. The subordinate clause starts with a conjunction like: because, although, when, if, since, until, while, unless.
I stayed home because I was sick. 🔊
Although it was cold, she went swimming. 🔊
When I grow up, I want to be a scientist. 🔊
If you study hard, you will do well. 🔊
She cried until she fell asleep. 🔊
💡 When the subordinate clause comes FIRST, put a comma after it. When it comes SECOND, no comma needed.
Practice Exercises
___ it was late, I kept reading. (although)
I bring an umbrella ___ it might rain. (because)
___ the bell rings, class is over. (when)
I'll help you ___ you help me. (if)
Verb Tenses: Past, Present & Future
English has three main tenses: PRESENT (now/always), PAST (already happened), FUTURE (will happen).
PRESENT: I eat breakfast every morning. / She reads books. 🔊
PAST: I ate breakfast at 7am. / She read all night. 🔊
FUTURE: I will eat breakfast soon. / She will read tonight. / She is going to read tonight. 🔊
💡 "Going to" = planned future. "Will" = decided in the moment or prediction.
Practice Exercises
She ___ (study) every night. (PRESENT)
They ___ (visit) the museum last week. (PAST)
I ___ (call) you tomorrow. (FUTURE)
He ___ (go) to the doctor after school. (planned FUTURE)
Comparative & Superlative Adjectives
Compare two things with -ER or MORE. Compare three+ things with -EST or MOST.
tall → taller → tallest 🔊
fast → faster → fastest 🔊
beautiful → more beautiful → most beautiful 🔊
good → better → best 🔊
bad → worse → worst 🔊
little → less → least 🔊
💡 Short adjectives (1–2 syllables): add -ER/-EST. Long adjectives (3+ syllables): use MORE/MOST.
Practice Exercises
This book is ___ (interesting) than that one. (MORE)
She is the ___ (fast) runner on the team.
That is the ___ (bad) movie I've ever seen.
A cheetah is ___ (quick) than a horse.

He gave every child in the kingdom one seed.

The Water Cycle (Science) ★★★ (Grade 3 Informational · ~160 words)
💡 建议先读懂英文原文，遇到不懂的句子点每句旁边的【译】按钮。整段翻译可复制文章后粘贴到 translate.google.com

Water is always moving around our planet in a cycle.
This cycle has four main stages.

The first stage is evaporation.
The sun heats water in oceans, lakes, and rivers.
The warm water turns into water vapor — tiny drops of water floating in the air.
You cannot see water vapor, but it is everywhere.

The second stage is condensation.
As water vapor rises, it cools down.
Cool air cannot hold as much water, so the vapor turns into tiny water droplets.
These droplets gather together to form clouds.

The third stage is precipitation.
When clouds collect too many water droplets, the water falls back to Earth.
It can fall as rain, snow, sleet, or hail — depending on the temperature.

The final stage is collection.
Water collects in rivers, lakes, and oceans.
Then the cycle begins again.

This process has been happening for billions of years,
which means the water you drink today may have once been inside a dinosaur!

Comprehension Questions
1. What causes evaporation?
Hint: the sun heats the water
2. What is condensation?
Hint: water vapor cools and turns into droplets that form clouds
3. Name three forms of precipitation.
Hint: rain, snow, sleet, or hail
4. What is a fun fact mentioned in the article?
Hint: the water you drink may have been inside a dinosaur

Modal Verbs · can, may, might, must, should, would
Modal verbs add meaning to the main verb. They express ability, permission, possibility, advice, or obligation.
ABILITY: I can swim. She can't drive yet. 🔊
PERMISSION: May I use the bathroom? You may go. 🔊
POSSIBILITY: It might rain. She might be home. 🔊
OBLIGATION: You must wear a seatbelt. 🔊
ADVICE: You should see a doctor. You shouldn't stay up so late. 🔊
POLITE REQUEST: Would you please help me? 🔊
💡 Modals NEVER change form: "She cans" ✗ → "She can" ✓. Always followed by base verb: "I should goes" ✗ → "I should go" ✓
Practice Exercises
You ___ eat so much sugar. It's bad for you. (advice)
___ I borrow your pen? (polite permission)
She ___ be at the library. I'm not sure. (possibility)
All students ___ be quiet during the exam. (obligation)
Using Evidence from Text: Quoting & Paraphrasing
In academic writing, you must support your ideas with EVIDENCE from the text. You can QUOTE (use exact words) or PARAPHRASE (say it in your own words).
DIRECT QUOTE: According to the text, "Ping walked in with an empty pot." 🔊
PARAPHRASE: The text explains that Ping's seed never grew. 🔊
ANALYSIS: This shows that Ping was honest, even when it was difficult. 🔊
SENTENCE FRAME: The evidence that supports this is "..." This means that... 🔊
💡 After every quote, EXPLAIN it. Never just drop a quote and move on. Say: "This shows..." or "This means..." or "This demonstrates..."
Practice Exercises
The text states, "___." This shows that Ping was courageous.
According to the article, ___. This evidence supports the idea that...

Active vs. Passive Voice
ACTIVE: Subject DOES the action. PASSIVE: Subject RECEIVES the action. Active is usually stronger and clearer.
ACTIVE: The dog bit the man. 🔊
PASSIVE: The man was bitten by the dog. 🔊
ACTIVE: Scientists discovered a new planet. 🔊
PASSIVE: A new planet was discovered by scientists. 🔊
USE PASSIVE WHEN: The doer is unknown (The window was broken.) or when the action is more important than who did it. 🔊
💡 Look for "was/were + past participle (eaten, broken, discovered)" → that's passive voice.
Practice Exercises
ACTIVE → PASSIVE: "Shakespeare wrote Hamlet." → "Hamlet ___ ___ by Shakespeare."
PASSIVE → ACTIVE: "The cake was eaten by Tim." → "Tim ___ the cake."
Is this active or passive? "The bridge was built in 1887."
Change to active: "Mistakes were made." → Who made them? Rewrite if possible.
Analyzing Author's Craft: HOW does the author write?
Authors make deliberate choices about: DICTION (word choice), SYNTAX (sentence structure), TONE (attitude), and LITERARY DEVICES. Analyzing craft means asking: WHY did the author write it this way?
SHORT sentences = urgency, tension. "She ran. She fell. She screamed." 🔊
LONG sentences = flowing, calm, descriptive detail that draws you in slowly. 🔊
WORD CHOICE: "He trudged" vs "He walked" — what's the difference in feeling? 🔊
REPETITION: "We shall fight... we shall never surrender." — creates emphasis, rhythm. 🔊
💡 ANALYSIS sentence frame: "The author's use of [device] in [line/passage] creates [effect], which suggests [meaning]."
Practice Exercises
The author uses the simile "quiet as a mouse" to suggest that the character is ___.
The author's choice of the word "trudged" instead of "walked" creates a feeling of ___.
Write an analysis sentence: The author uses ___ to show/create ___.

✍️ Argumentative Essay with Counterclaim
A strong argument admits the other side exists, then explains why your side is still stronger. Structure: Hook → Thesis → Argument 1 → Argument 2 → COUNTERCLAIM + REBUTTAL → Conclusion.
Model Example:
Should students be allowed to use smartphones in school?

Every year, millions of students carry a supercomputer in their pocket —
but are banned from using it in class.
I argue that high school students should be allowed to use smartphones in school,
with appropriate guidelines.

First, smartphones are powerful learning tools.
Students can access encyclopedias, calculators, and research databases instantly.
According to a 2022 study, students who used phones for approved educational tasks
scored 15% higher on information-retention tests.

Second, preparing students for the modern workplace requires digital literacy.
In almost every career today, people use devices to communicate, research, and collaborate.
Banning phones teaches students that technology is the enemy, not a tool to be mastered.

Some people argue that phones distract students and lower grades.
This is a valid concern.
However, the solution is not a total ban, but clear policies.
Schools that taught students to use phones responsibly
actually reported fewer distractions than schools with complete bans.

In conclusion, with the right guidelines, smartphones can transform classrooms.
The question is not whether to allow technology — it is how to use it wisely.

Sentence Variety — The Art of Mixing Sentence Types
Good writers vary their sentence structure. Using only simple sentences sounds choppy. Using only complex sentences sounds exhausting. Mix them for rhythm and emphasis.
ALL SIMPLE (boring): She ran. She fell. She cried. She got up. 🔊
ALL COMPLEX (exhausting): Although she was running as fast as she could because she was late, she fell, which caused her to cry, but she recovered... 🔊
VARIED (effective): She ran. She was nearly there — just a hundred more feet. Then she fell. For a moment, she lay on the ground, her knee throbbing. She took one breath, then another. And then she got up. 🔊
💡 ONE short sentence after a long one = EMPHASIS. Short = shock. Long sentences build up tension until the short sentence delivers the punch.

Rhetorical Analysis — How to Analyze a Text
Rhetorical analysis asks: WHO is speaking? To WHOM? For what PURPOSE? With what EFFECT? Use SOAPS: Speaker, Occasion, Audience, Purpose, Subject.
SPEAKER: Who wrote it? What are their credentials? Bias? 🔊
OCCASION: When/why was it written? What was happening in the world? 🔊
AUDIENCE: Who is it written for? How does that shape the writing? 🔊
PURPOSE: Persuade? Inform? Entertain? Inspire? 🔊
SUBJECT: What is it literally about? 🔊
💡 ANALYSIS FRAME: "In [title], [author] uses [rhetorical strategy] to [purpose], which [effect on audience]."

I Have a Dream — Excerpt (Martin Luther King Jr., 1963) ★★★★★ (Grade 6 Rhetoric · Primary Source)
💡 建议先读懂英文原文，遇到不懂的句子点每句旁边的【译】按钮。整段翻译可复制文章后粘贴到 translate.google.com

I say to you today, my friends, so even though we face the difficulties of today and tomorrow, I still have a dream.
It is a dream deeply rooted in the American dream.

I have a dream that one day this nation will rise up and live out the true meaning of its creed:
"We hold these truths to be self-evident, that all men are created equal."

I have a dream that one day on the red hills of Georgia, the sons of former slaves and the sons of former slave owners will be able to sit down together at the table of brotherhood.

I have a dream that my four little children will one day live in a nation where they will not be judged by the color of their skin but by the content of their character.

I have a dream today!

And when this happens, and when we allow freedom to ring, when we let it ring from every village and every hamlet, from every state and every city, we will be able to speed up that day when all of God's children — Black men and white men, Jews and Gentiles, Protestants and Catholics — will be able to join hands and sing in the words of the old Negro spiritual: "Free at last! Free at last! Thank God Almighty, we are free at last!"

Comprehension Questions
1. What rhetorical device is repeated throughout this speech? What effect does it create?
Hint: anaphora: "I have a dream..." — creates rhythm, emphasis, memorability
2. Identify one example of PATHOS (emotional appeal). Why is it effective?
Hint: "my four little children" — personal, human, universal
3. What is King's central CLAIM in this speech?
Hint: all people should be equal and free, regardless of race
4. King quotes the Declaration of Independence: "all men are created equal." Why? (What appeal is this?)
Hint: Logos/Ethos — using America's own foundational values to support his argument
5. Write a SOAPS analysis for this speech in 5 sentences.
Hint: Speaker, Occasion, Audience, Purpose, Subject
💬 Speaking & Listening
A Formal Debate 🏛️
Moderator Today's debate: "Social media does more harm than good to teenagers." Team A argues FOR. Team B argues AGAINST. Team A, please present. 🔊
Team A Thank you. We claim that social media causes significant harm to teenagers. According to the American Psychological Association, teens who use social media more than 3 hours daily report 60% higher rates of depression and anxiety. This is clear evidence of harm. 🔊
Moderator Team B, your response. 🔊
Team B We respectfully disagree. While we acknowledge that excessive use can be harmful, social media also connects isolated teenagers to support communities. A 2021 study found that LGBTQ+ teens who used online communities reported 40% less loneliness. The problem is misuse, not the technology itself. 🔊
Team A That's a false equivalence. We concede that some teens benefit. However, the data shows that the majority of users experience negative effects. The benefits do not outweigh the harms. 🔊
Team B We challenge that framing. "Majority" needs a source. Furthermore, restricting social media without teaching responsible use doesn't solve the problem — it simply removes a tool teens will find anyway. 🔊
Moderator Strong arguments on both sides. Let's move to the cross-examination. 🔊
✏️ Debate phrases: "We respectfully disagree because..." / "We concede that..., however..." / "The data shows..." / "I challenge that claim because..." / "Can you provide a source for that?"

✍️ Rhetorical Analysis Essay
Choose a speech, advertisement, or article. Analyze HOW the author persuades — not just WHAT they say. Use SOAPS + Ethos/Logos/Pathos framework.
Model Example:
Analysis of "I Have a Dream" by Martin Luther King Jr.

In 1963, America stood at a crossroads.
One hundred years after the Emancipation Proclamation, Black Americans still faced legal segregation, police violence, and systemic inequality.
On August 28th, Martin Luther King Jr. stood before 250,000 people in Washington D.C. and delivered one of the most powerful speeches in history.
In "I Have a Dream," King masterfully employs anaphora, pathos, and strategic allusion to unite a divided nation behind the cause of racial equality.

King's most powerful rhetorical strategy is anaphora — the deliberate repetition of the phrase "I have a dream."
This repetition appears eight times in the final section, creating a hypnotic rhythm that transforms political speech into something closer to music or prayer.
The effect on the audience is cumulative: each repetition builds emotional momentum, making the dream feel not just possible but inevitable.

King also uses pathos with extraordinary precision.
When he speaks of "my four little children," he transforms an abstract political argument into a human reality every parent understands.
This appeal to emotion does not manipulate — it clarifies.
It reminds the audience that civil rights are not a political issue but a human one.

Ultimately, King's speech endures because it argues not from anger but from love.
Its power lies in its refusal to abandon the American dream — instead, it holds America accountable to its own values.
That is the highest form of rhetoric: making your audience want to be better.
